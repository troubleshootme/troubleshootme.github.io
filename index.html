💰 Margin Discipline: Convert Negotiating Room Into Retained Contribution

  1. Set the buy ceiling before negotiating.

    • Use: Maximum carrier buy = customer revenue − other direct shipment costs − required contribution − approved risk reserve.
    • Include unrecovered handling, detention, tracking or special-service costs where applicable.
    • Avoid counting expenses twice when the carrier’s complete offer already includes them.
    • If qualified coverage exceeds the ceiling, obtain a price or service adjustment—or decline the commitment.

  2. Do not anchor your margin to the posted/paid difference.

    • Specialized’s $0.11/mile and heavy haul’s $0.09/mile posted-over-paid differences are negotiating signals.
    • They are not evidence that a particular shipper will pay the posted average or that a particular carrier will accept the paid average.
    • Price from executable shipment economics, not assumed benchmark capture.

  3. Price equipment commitment, not just loaded distance.

    • A short movement that consumes the loading day can require more compensation than mileage suggests.
    • Include loading duration, unloading uncertainty, remaining driver hours and the next feasible pickup.
    • Never discount against an unconfirmed reload.

  4. Keep conditional reserves separate from actual costs.

    • A risk reserve supports booking approval; it is not automatically an expense.
    • Reconcile actual contribution after delivery and all charges are known.
    • Track which risks materialize so future quotes become more precise rather than permanently padded.
