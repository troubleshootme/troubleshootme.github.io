⏱️ First 90 Minutes: Protect the Book, Then Hunt

  1. Rank uncovered freight by consequence and latest workable dispatch.

    • Start with production-critical deliveries, temperature-sensitive shipments, fixed appointments and permit-dependent moves.
    • Record the maximum authorized carrier cost, customer escalation deadline and backup operating plan.
    • Cover critical freight when an acceptable executable offer appears; do not expose it to failure while searching for a marginal saving.

  2. Refresh uncommitted quotes against identical scope.

    • Request actual truck location, trailer specification, preceding delivery status and HOS (Hours of Service) availability.
    • Specify fuel treatment, loading method, stops, detention, tarping, permits and escorts.
    • Compare complete offers. A lower base quote with unresolved exclusions is not necessarily cheaper.

  3. Call proven, nearby carriers before broadcasting widely.

    • Positioned equipment reduces empty miles and scheduling uncertainty.
    • Ask: “What operating change would improve your price—earlier release, a wider window or confirmed fast unloading?”
    • Trade only flexibility the customer has approved, and capture savings only after the carrier revises its commitment.

  4. Release prospecting effort once imminent coverage is controlled.

    • Begin with existing industrial customers whose requirements match your carrier network.
    • For new opportunities, obtain shipment dimensions, actual addresses, release status and receiving windows before undertaking extensive sourcing.
    • End discovery calls with a specific next step: a complete shipment specification or permission to quote a released load.

💰 Partial Freight: The Most Important Quoting Trap Today

  • Treat the adverse spread as a costing warning.

    • Paid pricing exceeds posted pricing by a calculated $0.10/mile.
    • That comparison is between category averages, not matched shipments; it does not establish the loss on any individual load.
    • Do not promise savings based solely on advertised partial pricing.

  • Confirm the space and sequence—not just the truck.

    • Verify dimensions, weight, stackability, usable deck or floor space, commodity compatibility and loading order.
    • Establish whether another customer’s stop can delay your delivery.
    • Document handling, detention, extra-stop and redelivery terms before quoting.

  • Separate partial truckload from conventional LTL (Less Than Truckload).

    • Terminal-based LTL and shared-truck partial service have different cost and service structures.
    • Compare complete delivered cost and realistic transit performance.
    • Use dedicated service when the consequences of delay or incompatibility outweigh the confirmed consolidation saving.
