💰 Buy the Complete Operating Plan

High fuel costs make truck positioning important—but the broker must avoid double-counting costs already included in a carrier’s offer.

At $6.372/gallon, an illustrative additional 100 empty miles at an assumed 6 miles per gallon costs approximately $106 in tractor fuel alone. Driver time, maintenance and equipment expense are additional; refrigeration fuel is separate.

  • Compare equivalent complete offers.

    • Match equipment, pickup commitment, fuel treatment, route assumptions and accessorial scope.
    • Use deadhead—empty travel to the shipment—to assess feasibility and negotiating leverage.
    • Do not add estimated positioning fuel again when it is already embedded in the complete carrier quote.

  • Count reload value only when executable.

    • Confirm the first delivery, travel to the next pickup, available driver hours and cargo compatibility.
    • A promising destination is not a committed reload.
    • Remove speculative reload savings when a release delay makes the next pickup unreachable.

  • Separate today’s fuel cost from prospective relief.

    • Declining fuel futures support watching for later pump-price relief, not immediately reducing carrier fuel recovery.
    • Apply customer surcharge changes according to the contract’s benchmark and reset schedule.
    • Do not promise a fixed retail decline or assume carriers’ costs move uniformly.

Decision example: A nearby truck with a somewhat higher complete quote can be preferable to a distant truck whose lower offer depends on flawless loading and receiving. Choose based on the additional cost versus documented failure exposure—not proximity alone.

Shipment contribution = customer revenue − complete carrier cost − other shipment-specific costs.

For uncertain moves, also evaluate expected unrecovered exception cost. Use comparable shipment history to estimate likelihood and severity; without that history, compare normal, delayed and failed-execution scenarios rather than inventing probabilities.
