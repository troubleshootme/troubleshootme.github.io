💰 Pricing and Negotiation: Sell Certainty Without Giving It Away

  1. Offer a protected option and a flexible option.

    • Protected: Price against accepted qualified capacity, confirmed appointments and written accessorial terms.
    • Flexible: Tie savings to a wider pickup window, faster unloading or an accepted positioning concession.
    • This gives budget-sensitive customers a real choice without silently transferring uncertainty to the brokerage.

  2. Shorten uncovered quote validity in aligned hot-and-rising markets.

    • Consider 30–60-minute validity for urgent Memphis, Birmingham, Montgomery, Portland and Spokane quotes where commercially practical.
    • State when capacity must be reconfirmed.
    • A valid quote is not a confirmed truck.

  3. Negotiate the carrier’s operating plan before the headline price.

    • Ask when the truck is empty, where the driver needs to finish, and which next appointment must be protected.
    • Home-time alignment and fast unloading can produce a concession that generic “loads are down” arguments will not.
    • Record any promised operating improvement so dispatch can actually deliver it.

  4. Keep fuel separate from local capacity economics.

    • National diesel is $5.844/gallon, following 14 consecutive daily declines.
    • Apply the customer’s contractual surcharge index and reset schedule.
    • Do not count fuel relief twice or assume it offsets empty miles, waiting or tight-origin purchasing.

  5. Price the complete commitment.

    • Include carrier purchase, known accessorials and shipment-specific contingency exposure.
    • For a gross-margin target: customer price = included shipment cost ÷ (1 − target gross-margin rate).
    • Avoid duplicating a contingency already included in an all-in carrier purchase.

Customer language: “Your pickup market is tight even though national listings are lower. We can protect the appointment with confirmed capacity, or test savings against a wider operating window and a usable destination reload.”
