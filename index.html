🚦 First 90 Minutes: Secure the Freight That Can Hurt You

  1. Rank uncovered loads by replacement difficulty and service consequence.

    • Cover temperature-sensitive freight, production-critical deliveries and hard-to-replace equipment first.
    • Assign an owner, carrier-buy ceiling and customer escalation deadline.
    • Separate genuinely urgent shipments from customers expressing urgency about flexible freight.

  2. Verify readiness before creating truck exposure.

    • Confirm commodity, weight, dimensions, loading method, written handling instructions and receiving availability.
    • Distinguish a covered shipment from a shipment actually ready to load.
    • Agree who pays waiting or cancellation costs if readiness changes.

  3. Refresh complete bids on comparable terms.

    • Confirm truck location, empty repositioning, legal driver hours, appointment feasibility, fuel treatment and accessorial charges.
    • Obtain competing qualified offers where time permits.
    • Do not use an incomplete low quote to pressure a carrier offering an executable service.

  4. Release remaining desk capacity to revenue work.

    • Start with existing customers whose freight matches positioned carriers.
    • Then prospect repeat Southeast agricultural and distribution movements.
    • Pursue complex specialized inquiries only where the likely contribution justifies the required desk time.

A useful prioritization measure is expected contribution per desk hour—not shipment revenue alone. A modest repeat movement can outperform a large, poorly specified project that consumes the entire morning.

🕒 Friday Reload Economics: Make the Connection Real

Do not credit a backhaul merely because a listing exists. A backhaul is return-direction freight that may improve the carrier’s round-trip economics.

  • Check the entire connection. Match expected unloading completion, travel to the next pickup, reload cutoff, trailer preparation and remaining legal driver hours.
  • Confirm acceptance—not just opening hours. A facility may remain open while refusing additional Friday pickups.
  • Verify compatibility. Food, chemicals, odor-sensitive cargo and different temperature requirements can make an apparent reload unusable.
  • Price the failed-connection case. Identify weekend waiting, alternative repositioning and the party responsible for each cost.
  • Keep the outbound movement economically sound. If the return shipment is unconfirmed, treat it as potential upside—not committed savings.

Illustrative decision: A carrier offers a lower Atlanta–Jacksonville price contingent on a Friday reload. If the receiver cannot confirm unloading before that reload’s cutoff, either secure a workable schedule or reprice the movement without the assumed return freight.

💰 Carrier Negotiation: Trade Certainty for Price

  1. Make total operating miles visible.

    • Deadhead means empty repositioning miles.
    • At $6.278/gallon, an illustrative 100 empty miles at an assumed 6 miles per gallon costs about $105 in tractor fuel alone.
    • Driver time, maintenance and refrigeration fuel are additional.
    • Use this to compare positioning—not to add carrier fuel costs again when already included in the quote.

  2. Remove a real expense before requesting a concession.

    • Test wider pickup windows, faster unloading, fewer stops or a better-positioned destination.
    • Obtain a revised complete price after the customer confirms the operating change.
    • Make detention terms and loading readiness explicit.

  3. Set the buying ceiling before becoming emotionally committed.

    Maximum carrier buy = customer revenue − other direct costs − required contribution − approved risk reserve.

    • Keep an internal risk reserve separate from an incurred expense.
    • Avoid double counting expenses already embedded in the carrier’s price.
    • If qualified offers exceed the ceiling, revise the service, obtain customer approval for an adjustment or decline the commitment.

  4. Separate fuel mechanics from discretionary negotiation.

    • Retail relief is underway, but the supplied futures direction differs across daily and five-day horizons.
    • Apply fuel-surcharge changes through the actual agreement’s benchmark and reset schedule.
    • Do not promise future fuel savings in a fixed quote.

Behavioral guardrail: A broker anchored to a national average may mistake a justified positioned-equipment premium for unreasonable resistance. A carrier anchored to earlier pricing may overlook the value of ready freight. Move the discussion to operating facts both parties can verify.
