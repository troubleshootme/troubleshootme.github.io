  1. Re-rank open shipments by failure consequence and replacement difficulty.

    • Elevate unbought van shipments quoted from unconfirmed purchase assumptions.
    • Assign a latest safe booking time, departure time and escalation owner to each exposed shipment.

  2. Refresh customer commitments before shopping indefinitely.

    • Separate firm commitments from estimates and quotes awaiting acceptance.
    • For new spot quotes, use a validity window appropriate to actual carrier availability.
    • Do not assume an existing customer commitment permits repricing; escalate uncovered exposure internally.

  3. Confirm the underlying truck.

    • Verify equipment location, preceding delivery, trailer suitability and available HOS (Hours of Service).
    • Ask when the truck will be empty and legally available—not simply when dispatch expects arrival.
    • Confirm that primary and backup offers do not represent the same underlying equipment.

  4. Clear commercial and identity controls before dispatch.

    • Verify applicable operating authority, insurance, contracting entity and authorized contacts.
    • Independently confirm changes to pickup authorization or payment instructions.

  5. Stop shopping when replacement risk exceeds likely savings.

    • Continue competition while executable alternatives and departure time remain.
    • Escalate when a lower offer introduces uncertain equipment, delayed arrival or qualification gaps.
    • Record why the selected carrier is operationally preferable, not merely where its bid ranked.

Buying principle: a cheap offer with unresolved readiness is not yet a cheaper shipment.

🚚 Van: Negotiate the Constraint, Not the Headline

Van deserves the largest adjustment in today’s procurement posture. Its paid-over-posted relationship warrants caution, but does not prove every lane is tight.

  • Request comparable bids. Give each carrier the same loading window, delivery commitment, weight, stops, tracking requirements and fuel/accessorial scope.
  • Identify what is driving the offer. Ask whether the constraint is empty mileage, Sunday pickup willingness, loading uncertainty, remaining driving hours or poor onward freight.
  • Exchange operational improvements for a better purchase. Verified product readiness, a wider customer-approved window or faster unloading may improve utilization more than repeated requests for a concession.
  • Keep service alternatives distinct. Price flexible standard service separately from a rigid appointment or reserved-capacity commitment. Do not sell premium certainty against an uncommitted truck.

Customer language: “We are checking qualified trucks against your actual appointment. We can compare a firm-window option with a flexible option, but posted averages alone are not a reliable purchase commitment.”

Psychological discipline: Customers may interpret fewer listings as cheaper transportation; carriers may anchor to their hardest recent load. Move both discussions toward current, equivalent, executable alternatives.
