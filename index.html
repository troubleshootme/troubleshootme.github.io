🧭 Savvy Broker's Playbook

🔑 Executive Signal Summary

  • Sell the opportunity, not a shortage story. Available loads are 130,498, up 13.5% from the comparable checkpoint, but the national average is $2.93/mi. The increase is concentrated in open-deck freight; it is not a reason to raise every van or reefer quote.
  • Protect the buy side first. Van averages $2.72/mi posted versus $2.80/mi paid; reefer averages $3.43/mi posted versus $3.52/mi paid. Those differences are not broker margins. With diesel at $6.439/gal, a distant truck can turn a promising sell into a poor load.
  • Treat quarter-end as an execution test. The best near-term advantage is a confirmed truck, workable appointments, and a quote that remains valid through the actual pickup window—not a persuasive national-market narrative.

🎯 Next-Shift Desk Order

  1. Clear at-risk commitments before prospecting. Reconfirm truck location, trailer suitability, driver availability, pickup readiness, and receiving cutoff. Flag shipments whose delivery plan depends on an unconfirmed appointment or reload.
  2. Refresh uncovered quotes against live carrier offers. Record the all-in carrier buy, customer sell, likely empty approach, fuel treatment, accessorials, and quote expiration. If pickup timing changes, recheck the buy before renewing the customer price.
  3. Work open-deck releases with a complete scope. Flatbed has 48,096 listings; heavy haul has 23,698. Ask for dimensions, weight, loading method, securement, site access, and required permits or escorts before promising a price or transit plan.
  4. Pursue Southeast freight shipment by shipment. Ask North Carolina produce shippers what is ready, how it must travel, and when it can be received. Ask Charlotte-area distribution customers for firm release and Atlanta receiving times. A seasonal prospect is valuable only when it becomes an executable shipment.

🚛 Where to Spend Carrier Calls

  • Van — buy the appointment, not the average. With 24,996 listings and paid rates above posted rates, test Charlotte → Atlanta with carriers already positioned near pickup. A carrier’s Atlanta reload may improve its offer, but do not price that saving in until the reload and both appointments are confirmed.
  • Reefer — establish the commodity specification first. With 8,699 listings and a $3.52/mi paid average, source Greenville → Atlanta sweet potatoes only after receiving the shipper’s temperature, ventilation, loading, and trailer instructions. Do not assume a particular reefer setting or pre-cooling requirement.
  • Flatbed — compete on certainty. Its $3.12/mi posted and $3.10/mi paid averages are close. Win by giving a qualified carrier an accurate scope and an achievable loading window, not by treating the aggregate difference as available margin.
  • Heavy haul and specialized — qualify before negotiating. Heavy haul averages $3.34/mi posted and $3.31/mi paid; specialized averages $3.12/mi posted and $3.10/mi paid. Equipment fit, operator qualifications, routing, and project timing can dominate either benchmark.
  • LTL (Less Than Truckload)/partial — preserve service options. 8,733 listings at $1.78/mi posted and $1.77/mi paid warrant a consolidation check when a customer has flexible transit. Confirm handling, coverage, and the full shipment charge rather than extrapolating a per-mile margin.

⚠️ Exceptions That Should Change the Plan

  • Flood-area dispatch: Verify current road conditions and facility access for southeastern Arizona, affected eastern Iowa and northern Illinois locations, and any Florida pickup near the St. Johns River warning. A flood warning is a reason to check the route—not evidence that a major highway is closed.
  • Carrier resistance: If multiple qualified, nearby carriers reject the same pickup window or require materially higher all-in buys, shorten quote validity and escalate coverage. If responses improve, use that advantage on repeat freight without assuming it applies to every lane.
  • Fuel and reload assumptions: Compare positioned, fuel-inclusive offers. Credit a return move only when its equipment, timing, and customer commitment are real; otherwise, the carrier still has an empty-mile problem.

📏 End-of-Shift Scorecard

  • Committed loads: Every pickup has a verified carrier, equipment match, and appointment plan.
  • Open quotes: Every time-sensitive quote has a current carrier-buy basis and an expiration.
  • Prospecting: Produce and distribution conversations produce specific releases and service requirements—not just expressions of interest.
  • Exceptions: Weather-exposed loads have an access check; planned reloads are confirmed or removed from the economics.
