  3. Separate a quoted truck from a dispatch-ready truck.

  4. Set a stop-shopping rule.

    • Continue competitive buying while qualified alternatives remain and the service window is protected.
    • Stop when further shopping risks the only feasible truck or the latest safe dispatch.
    • Require approval when a purchase exceeds the shipment’s contribution floor or leaves a material operating dependency unresolved.

The hidden exposure today is an old purchase assumption attached to a firm customer promise. Correct that before expanding the book.

🏗️ Open Deck and Partials: Capture Growth Without Buying Hidden Scope

  • Flatbed: standardize the bid package. Confirm dimensions, weight, trailer configuration, securement, tarping and loading/unloading equipment. Equal posted and paid averages do not mean shipment-level requirements are interchangeable.
  • Heavy haul: make feasibility the first sale. Resolve axle configuration, route approval, escorts, permits and jurisdiction-specific movement windows before an unconditional service commitment. Check whether changing equipment would affect approvals.
  • Specialized: sell the capability you can actually source. Build a short list by trailer subtype, operator qualification and geography. Escalate moves with only one executable provider before promising recovery flexibility.
  • LTL (Less Than Truckload)/partial: distinguish the service models. A carrier-network LTL quote and a truckload partial arrangement have different handling, rating and transit obligations. Confirm dimensions, stackability, stops, custody and claims terms.

Consolidation gate: Accept a combined move only when the purchase saving survives additional stops, handling, waiting and delivery risk. More freight on one trailer is not automatically better economics.
