  • Make the next load part of the negotiation. At $6.514/gal diesel, a nearby pickup and credible reload can be more persuasive to a carrier than a nominally higher rate followed by an empty move.

🎯 Today’s Desk Priorities

  1. Cover exposed commitments first. Identify uncovered van, produce, and permit-dependent loads. Get a carrier-specific buy and realistic pickup time before giving the shipper a firm commitment.
  2. Call carriers already approaching your origins. Ask where they will empty, when the trailer will be ready, and what freight they need after delivery. A proposed “backhaul” discount has no value unless the next move works for that carrier.
  3. Sell certainty to quarter-end shippers. Offer a clear pickup plan, appointment status, and written exception terms. Shippers under deadline pressure may value a dependable answer more than an optimistic opening quote.
  4. Clear flood-exposed exceptions before dispatch. Confirm the facility’s entrance, staging area, and final-mile route with both the facility and driver. Do not infer a highway or bridge closure from a river warning.

💵 Equipment Buying Playbook

  • Dry van — defend the buy. With 22,042 loads and paid rates $0.09/mi above posted, source locally and check dock time and destination options before quoting. On Atlanta → Charlotte, price the complete turn: a short loaded run can lose its economics in loading delays or an empty return.
  • Reefer — test the apparent opportunity. With 8,477 loads and paid rates $0.15/mi below posted, look for equipment delivering into a consumer market and needing a compatible next move. For Raleigh → Atlanta, verify trailer condition, commodity requirements, pre-cool, setpoint, and appointments before offering either a produce load or a dry-freight reload. Harvest-origin capacity may still command a premium.
  • Flatbed — pursue repeatable, fully scoped freight. Its 49,676 loads make it the largest visible category; $3.06/mi posted and $3.07/mi paid are nearly aligned. Win on accurate dimensions, loading method, securement, tarping, and access—not an assumed rate concession.
  • Heavy haul and specialized — buy execution, not a spread. Heavy haul has 23,856 loads at $3.22/mi posted and $3.11/mi paid; specialized has 16,246 at $2.98/mi posted and $2.89/mi paid. Confirm trailer fit and handling requirements. For heavy haul, establish the permitted route, escorts, restrictions, and flood-sensitive access before promising delivery.
  • LTL (Less Than Truckload)/partial — present a service choice. Its 8,674 loads show $1.73/mi posted and paid. Offer consolidation where shipment size and transit flexibility justify it, while explaining handling, appointment, and liability differences from a dedicated truck.

Quote discipline: Keep the shipper bill, confirmed carrier buy, and expected gross margin separate. State whether fuel is included or adjusted, and obtain approval terms for detention, layover, extra stops, rerouting, permits, and escorts.
