📈 Rate Intelligence Brief: Fuel Cost Compression and Carrier Premiums

The rapid surge in the national AAA diesel average to $6.204 per gallon has fundamentally altered the spot market rate structure today. We are observing a significant compression in the spread between posted and paid rates, with carriers successfully demanding premiums on key equipment types. For dry vans, the average paid rate of $2.72/mile exceeds the posted rate of $2.67/mile by $0.05/mile, indicating that carriers are successfully passing on fuel costs to brokers. This trend is even more pronounced in the reefer sector, where paid rates average $3.52/mile compared to posted rates of $3.31/mile, representing an aggressive $0.21/mile carrier premium. This rate velocity is driven by carrier resistance to deadhead. With fuel costs exceeding $6.20 per gallon, a 100-mile deadhead cost carriers approximately $100 in fuel alone, making empty miles economically unviable. As a result, carriers are rejecting loads that require significant repositioning, forcing brokers to source highly localized capacity or pay substantial rate premiums to cover deadhead miles. Brokers must adjust their quoting models immediately to reflect this new reality, as historical rate averages from even a week ago are no longer accurate indicators of current market costs.

💵 Pricing strategy that protects margin today

  • Separate four components in your internal quote

    • Linehaul
    • Fuel
    • Dwell / detention exposure
    • Weather / routing risk

  • Treat quote validity as a weapon

    • In weather-affected or fuel-sensitive origins, shorten quote life.
    • A quote that sits too long in this market becomes a free option for the customer and a liability for the broker.

  • Pay for proximity, not theory

    • The theoretical cheap truck often becomes the expensive truck once you add:
    • deadhead
    • late pickup
    • layover risk
    • re-cover cost

  • Use practical shipper language

    • “Fuel is shrinking truck radius, so local capacity is pricing ahead of national averages.”
    • “We can protect service better by locking a nearby truck now than by chasing a cheaper reopen later.”
    • “Today’s lowest quote is often tomorrow’s service failure.”
