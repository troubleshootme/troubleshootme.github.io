🧭 Savvy Broker's Playbook

🔑 Executive Signal Summary

  • Sell by equipment, not by the headline. Available loads reached 130,498, up 13.5%, but flatbed and heavy haul account for 71,794 listings. That is an open-deck prospecting signal—not evidence that van or reefer capacity has tightened nationwide.
  • Protect the carrier buy before promising quarter-end service. Van paid averages exceed posted averages by $0.08/mi; reefer by $0.09/mi. Those are market snapshots, not broker margins or lane quotes. A pickup window, trailer requirement, or distant truck can change the actual buy substantially.
  • Make positioning part of every negotiation. At $6.439/gal diesel, a 100-mile empty approach costs roughly $92 in fuel alone for a truck averaging 7 miles per gallon. A lower linehaul offer from a distant carrier may be the more expensive—and less reliable—coverage choice.

🎯 The Next-Shift Desk Order

  1. Clear committed freight first. Reconfirm truck location, trailer fit, driver availability, pickup readiness, and receiving appointment. For quarter-end releases, ask customers which shipments are firm and which depend on inventory or production finishing overnight.
  2. Run two distinct sales queues. Approach construction and industrial accounts for specified flatbed and heavy-haul releases. Separately, approach Southeast produce and distribution accounts for shipment-ready van and temperature-sensitive freight. Do not pitch one market’s listing surge as proof of scarcity in the other.
  3. Quote from a complete trip. Record the fuel-inclusive carrier buy, empty approach, permits or accessorials where applicable, customer sell, expected gross margin, and quote expiration. Refresh an uncommitted buy when the pickup window or equipment instructions change.
  4. Keep a qualified backup—not just a second phone number. For priority freight, identify another carrier whose equipment, location, and available hours actually work. Tell the customer what is confirmed versus still being sourced.

🚛 Where to Spend Coverage Time

  • Flatbed and heavy haul—pursue the release, then scope it. Flatbed shows 48,096 loads, with $3.12/mi posted and $3.10/mi paid; heavy haul shows 23,698 loads, with $3.34/mi posted and $3.31/mi paid. Ask for dimensions, weight, loading method, securement, site access, and—in heavy haul—route, permit, and escort requirements before offering a fixed price. Small national posted-versus-paid gaps are not executable margins.
  • Reefer—prioritize equipment fit over a generic rate. 8,699 loads are listed, with $3.43/mi posted and $3.52/mi paid. For North Carolina sweet potatoes bound for Atlanta, obtain the shipper’s written temperature, ventilation, and trailer instructions; do not assume refrigeration or pre-cooling is required. Then source trucks close to the actual origin and loading window.
  • Van—sell appointment reliability. 24,996 loads are listed, with $2.72/mi posted and $2.80/mi paid. On Atlanta–Charlotte distribution freight, a nearby truck with a workable delivery appointment may be worth more than a cheaper offer that depends on a long empty approach.
  • Specialized and LTL/partial—verify the service before the saving. Specialized lists 16,276 loads at $3.12/mi posted and $3.10/mi paid; LTL/partial (Less Than Truckload) lists 8,733 loads at $1.78/mi posted and $1.77/mi paid. Check operator and trailer qualifications for specialized moves; for partials, compare the full charge, handling, compatibility, and delivery commitment rather than mileage averages.

🧭 A Better Southeast Play Than “Find a Backhaul”

Source Wilson, North Carolina → Atlanta produce and Atlanta → Charlotte distribution as independent shipments first. Only present them as a carrier sequence after both releases, trailer requirements, driving time, and appointments align. Otherwise, an assumed reload can quietly subsidize the first quote and put both customers’ pickups at risk.

Use positioning to negotiate without bluffing: ask a carrier where its truck will finish, when it can load, and where its driver needs to go next. That reveals whether your freight solves a real routing problem. Credit a future reload in today’s economics only when that reload is confirmed and compatible.

🌦️ Exceptions Worth an Early Dispatch Check

  • Southeastern Arizona: Confirm the facility approach and current route conditions for pickups near the warned I-10 corridor. Do not represent I-10 as closed without a route-specific restriction.
  • Northern Illinois: Check low-lying approaches near Russell and Gurnee before dispatch. The Des Plaines River warning is a local-access concern, not evidence of an I-94 closure.
  • Weather-exposed appointments: Give the shipper and receiver an updated arrival plan when access is uncertain; document any changed window before committing the carrier to it.

📍 Close-of-Shift Scorecard

Judge the desk by secured execution, not listings or call volume: committed loads with a qualified carrier and confirmed appointments; open quotes backed by a current fuel-inclusive buy; uncovered pickups with a viable backup; and weather-exposed loads with a verified access plan. Reprice when qualified carriers repeatedly decline the same shipment or their live buys rise—not merely because a national count moved.
