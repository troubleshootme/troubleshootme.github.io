🚛 Equipment Tactics: Spend Attention Where It Changes the Outcome

  1. Heavy haul: make the quote scope complete before making the price firm.

    • Obtain dimensions, weight, axle requirements, loading method and exact origin and destination.
    • Verify the proposed tractor-trailer configuration, operator qualifications, escorts, permits and legal movement windows.
    • Confirm whether permit applications depend on final equipment identification; a replacement truck may require more than a dispatch change.
    • State quote validity and unresolved conditions clearly. Do not conceal permit or access uncertainty inside an apparently firm all-in promise.

  2. Van: negotiate the operating plan, not just the mileage rate.

    • Compare offers using identical pickup readiness, delivery appointments and fuel treatment.
    • Ask carriers whether a wider loading window or a different receiving appointment produces a real improvement.
    • Distinguish genuine procurement pressure from a shipment that is simply unattractive because of waiting, deadhead or poor release timing.

  3. Reefer: protect readiness while testing the market.

    • Confirm prior cargo, cleaning requirements, temperature capability and monitoring expectations.
    • Obtain written temperature and ventilation instructions for sweet potatoes; do not substitute a generic chilled-produce setting.
    • Treat the October commodity list as seasonal prospecting context. The supplied “spring transition” wording is not applicable to this late-October operating plan.
    • Do not equate produce origins with available empty trucks. Validate positioning and return-freight compatibility independently.

  4. Flatbed and specialized: remove scope ambiguity before negotiating.

    • Specify trailer subtype, dimensions, securement, tarping, loading equipment and unloading responsibility.
    • Ask what is included in each bid and what operating change would trigger additional charges.
    • A cheaper offer with incomplete scope is not a comparable purchase.

  5. LTL (Less Than Truckload)/partial: calculate the complete shipment cost.

    • Confirm dimensions, weight, stackability, commodity compatibility and handling limitations.
    • For network LTL, include the carrier’s applicable rating basis and accessorials; a mileage average is not a shipment quote.
    • For partial truckload, include stop sequencing, detours, appointment compatibility and driver hours.
    • Consolidate only when the savings survive the added handling and service exposure.
