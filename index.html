⏱️ First 90 Minutes: Build a Bookable Shipment List

  1. Rank uncovered freight by the latest safe booking time.

    • Put temperature-sensitive freight, fixed receiving appointments, flood-exposed movements and permit-dependent loads first.
    • Identify trucks whose availability depends on an unfinished delivery.
    • Assign a named owner, escalation deadline and qualified recovery option to each exposed shipment.

  2. Confirm four facts before negotiating price.

    • Exact pickup location and actual freight-ready time.
    • Written equipment and commodity requirements.
    • Receiver appointment, staffing and usable truck access.
    • Carrier location, equipment readiness and remaining HOS (Hours of Service).

  3. Separate executable capacity from conditional offers.

    • A truck awaiting unloading, washout or a driver assignment is not equivalent to a ready truck nearby.
    • Record the condition attached to each offer and when it must be resolved.
    • Do not tell a customer coverage is firm while a critical operating dependency remains open.

  4. Set an explicit stop-shopping rule.

    • Continue shopping only while expected additional savings exceed the incremental risk of delay, replacement expense and service failure.
    • Use the desk’s comparable operating history—not invented probabilities.
    • Once an acceptable carrier commitment is made, honor it under the agreed terms.

The best procurement decision is not necessarily the lowest quote; it is the lowest defensible total cost of successful execution.

🧊 Reefer: Buy Readiness, Not Just a Trailer

  1. Treat the paid-over-posted relationship as an early-warning signal.

    • It may reflect difficult coverage, urgent freight or a more expensive shipment mix.
    • Confirm the local picture with comparable carrier offers, acceptance and time to cover.
    • For rigid appointments, secure the first qualified offer that meets the shipment’s approved cost and service requirements.

  2. Verify the equipment transition before counting a reload.

    • Confirm preceding cargo, unloading completion, cleaning requirements, equipment condition and temperature instructions.
    • Check whether washout facilities are available and whether preparation fits the next pickup.
    • Credit backhaul savings only after the reload is compatible and executable; do not assign the same reload to multiple trucks.

  3. Do not apply generic cold-produce instructions to sweet potatoes.

    • Obtain written temperature, ventilation, staging and monitoring requirements.
    • Sweet potatoes are chilling-sensitive; “produce” does not automatically mean conventional cold-produce settings.
    • Select equipment according to the shipper’s specifications rather than a seasonal label.

  4. Shorten quote validity when replacement exposure is unresolved.

    • State when the offer expires and whether it depends on identified equipment remaining available.
    • Obtain customer approval before assuming accessorial responsibility.
    • Do not promise an open-ended fixed price against an unconfirmed future truck.

Carrier negotiation: “What must happen before this equipment is ready, and which customer-approved change would improve your total offer?”

That question exposes operational waste without asking a dependable carrier to absorb an unworkable schedule.

⛽ Fuel Economics: Negotiate Empty Miles Before Loaded-Mile Price

  1. Calculate positioning cost explicitly.

    • At $6.347/gallon, a truck averaging 6 miles per gallon spends approximately $106 in diesel for an additional 100 empty miles.
    • This illustration excludes driver time, maintenance, tolls and other operating costs.
    • A farther-away truck can lose a nominal pricing advantage before reaching pickup.

  2. Compare complete offers on the same basis.

    • Confirm whether fuel is included, which accessorials are excluded and how long the offer remains valid.
    • Evaluate positioning time, appointment fit and likely equipment release—not only loaded-mile pricing.
    • Do not add a fuel calculation to an all-in carrier quote and count the expense twice.

  3. Align customer recovery with carrier commitments.

    • Check the customer’s fuel benchmark, reset schedule and governing shipment date.
    • Identify fixed carrier purchases exposed to a customer surcharge reset before movement.
    • Lower futures do not guarantee immediate retail relief or an equivalent carrier concession.

  4. Trade certainty for price only when the benefit is real.

    • Confirmed freight readiness, reduced waiting and a workable next movement may improve carrier economics.
    • Offer only changes the customer has authorized.
    • Do not use an unconfirmed reload or unrealistically short unloading estimate to make the purchase appear viable.
