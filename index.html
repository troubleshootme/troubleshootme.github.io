💵 Protect Contribution, Not Just the Quoted Spread

Diesel at $6.23/gallon keeps empty positioning expensive despite the established retail decline.

  • Quantify repositioning before widening the search. At an illustrative 6 miles per gallon, 100 empty miles consume approximately $104 of fuel alone, before driver time, maintenance or lost utilization. A lower carrier quote can disappear once positioning is included.
  • Separate fuel adjustments from transportation pricing. Apply the agreed surcharge index and reset schedule. Do not promise an immediate all-in reduction simply because retail fuel is easing.
  • Evaluate shipment contribution. Subtract carrier compensation and expected unrecovered execution costs from customer revenue. Include foreseeable detention, weekend holding, handling and recovery exposure.
  • Trade operational certainty for price. A ready shipment, realistic loading window and reachable receiving contact can be more valuable to a carrier than an unsupported promise of future volume.

Customer psychology matters: In hot origins, lead with a specific coverage plan and quote expiration—not an alarmist shortage narrative. In cool origins, offer documented savings without promising that today’s favorable procurement will persist indefinitely.

Carrier psychology matters too: Ask what makes the shipment unattractive before repeatedly asking for a lower number. The obstacle may be delivery timing or unpaid waiting, not the transportation rate itself.
