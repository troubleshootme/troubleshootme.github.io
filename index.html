⏱️ First 90 Minutes: Buy Down Risk Before Buying Down Price

  1. Rank uncovered shipments by consequence, not revenue alone.

    • Put production-critical deliveries, temperature-sensitive cargo, fixed receiving appointments and permit-dependent moves first.
    • Record the latest safe booking decision, a qualified backup and the customer escalation deadline.
    • Book when the expected cost of waiting exceeds the plausible procurement saving. Consider replacement coverage, missed receiving, cargo exposure and recovery labor—not just the initial carrier quote.

  2. Separate confirmed trucks from conditional trucks.

    • Verify current location, preceding delivery, trailer specification and HOS (Hours of Service).
    • Identify dependencies such as an uncertain unload, product release, weather clearance or permit approval.
    • A truck waiting for its previous customer to unload is not equivalent to a truck ready to dispatch.

  3. Run a controlled refresh on flexible freight.

    • Give suitable carriers identical shipment specifications and request complete trip pricing.
    • Compare the required appointment with a customer-approved flexible alternative.
    • Honor existing carrier agreements. Changing national averages are not grounds to renegotiate a booked commitment.

  4. Put an expiration and an owner on every unresolved assumption.

    • Assign responsibility for release confirmation, receiving approval, access checks and backup sourcing.
    • Keep conditional quotes clearly labeled until those dependencies are resolved.
    • Shorten quote validity where equipment positioning or weekend access can materially change.

Illustrative decision: A cheaper truck that cannot confirm Sunday receiving may consume Monday availability. A higher initial offer with confirmed unloading and legal onward availability can produce the better shipment contribution after waiting and recovery costs.
