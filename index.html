💰 Negotiate the Constraint—not the Color

Build the customer quote from a feasible carrier purchase, explicit service exposure and required contribution. Do not multiply a lane rate by the map’s percentage change.

  • Shorten exposure where density and momentum agree. For uncovered Memphis, Huntsville, Rochester and El Centro freight, consider 30–60-minute quote validity when commercially practical. Refresh the carrier purchase after expiration.
  • Trade flexibility before repeating the same counteroffer. Ask whether cost is driven by waiting, empty miles, destination position or pickup timing. An authorized appointment change may save more than another round of rate pressure.
  • Keep fuel separate. Diesel is $6.036/gallon. Apply the agreement’s benchmark and reset schedule; do not count fuel relief twice through both linehaul and surcharge reductions.
  • Use falling prices without predicting a rebound. In Phoenix, Richmond and Birmingham, seek competitive offers while preserving a qualified backup. Hot density does not make every high quote reasonable.
  • Counter behavioral anchoring with executable choices. Carriers may remember stronger pricing; customers may cite national softness. Present a confirmed pickup option and a separately priced flexible alternative rather than arguing about the national market.
  • Verify compliance without manufacturing scarcity. The removal of 133 electronic logging devices (ELDs) warrants checking device status and transition readiness. It does not establish 133 lost trucks—or a measurable national capacity reduction.

Customer language: “Your origin is tightening even though the national picture is mixed. This option protects the required pickup; this alternative tests a lower cost with your approved scheduling flexibility.”
