🛡️ Quote and Carrier Controls

  • Separate three numbers: the shipper’s bill, the confirmed carrier buy, and the resulting gross margin. Posted and paid board averages compare carrier-market prices; neither tells you your brokerage margin.
  • Write fuel and exception terms before dispatch. State whether the quote is all-in or has a fuel adjustment, and who approves and pays detention, layover, tarping, extra stops, rerouting, permits, and escorts.
  • Vet for execution, not headlines. Confirm active authority, insurance, equipment, driver location, dispatch responsiveness, and ability to complete the planned route. Apply requirements currently in force; a proposed regulatory change is not, by itself, evidence of capacity removed today.
  • Give carriers operational certainty. A precise pickup, honest dock expectations, a workable reload, and payment terms your brokerage can actually honor are stronger closing tools than an optimistic rate posting.
