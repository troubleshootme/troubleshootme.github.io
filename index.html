⏱️ First 90 Minutes: Protect Commitments Before Expanding the Book

  1. Sort open freight by replacement difficulty and consequence.

    • Put temperature-sensitive, permit-dependent, unusual-equipment and fixed-appointment shipments first.
    • Identify customer commitments without confirmed carrier purchases.
    • Assign each exposed shipment a booking deadline, latest feasible departure and escalation owner.

  2. Separate “quoted,” “accepted” and “dispatch-ready.”

    • Confirm truck location, preceding delivery, equipment readiness and driver HOS (Hours of Service).
    • Verify when the truck can legally reach the actual loading address.
    • Ensure competing offers and backups do not represent the same underlying truck.

  3. Standardize the bid before comparing prices.

    • Give every carrier the same weight, dimensions, stops, loading requirements, appointment windows and fuel/accessorial treatment.
    • Resolve exclusions before accepting an apparently lower purchase.
    • Confirm authority, insurance, authorized contacts and pickup identity through established controls.

  4. Set a stopping rule for shopping.

    • Continue bidding while qualified alternatives and departure time remain.
    • Stop when further shopping threatens pickup feasibility or leaves only conditional coverage.
    • Escalate an exposed shipment early rather than conceal it behind an optimistic carrier estimate.

The cheapest truck is not the lowest-cost purchase if it requires recovery, misses receiving or generates unrecoverable accessorials.

🧩 Partial Versus Dedicated: Compare the Whole Shipment

A lower mileage quote does not necessarily mean a lower shipment cost.

  • Build the shared-space total. Include minimum charges, pickup and delivery charges, additional handling, transfer exposure, appointment requirements and applicable accessorials.
  • Distinguish the service model. Network LTL and truckload partial service have different classification, handling and routing considerations. Confirm dimensions, linear feet, stackability and freight classification where applicable.
  • Obtain an equivalent dedicated alternative. Match the actual locations, service windows, equipment and handling requirements.
  • Separate cash cost from execution risk. A dedicated truck can reduce handling and sequencing exposure, but it is not automatically faster or safer. Confirm its actual schedule.

Illustrative decision: A large, nonstackable partial must reach a fixed receiving appointment. The shared-space option requires another pickup with uncertain release time. Even if its quoted transportation price is lower, the extra stop may consume the delivery buffer. Obtain a dedicated bid before committing—and compare the full service, not national per-mile averages.

Decision rule: Choose the lower-cost option that genuinely satisfies the service requirement. Where the cheaper option introduces material uncertainty, obtain explicit customer approval rather than quietly changing the service purchased.

🏗️ Specialized and Open-Deck Freight: Compete Precisely

  • Specialized—investigate the apparent negotiating room. Seek repeat bids from carriers with the required equipment, operator qualifications and cargo coverage. Ask what changed when offers differ materially: scope, positioning, availability or exclusions.
  • Flatbed—remove ambiguity before negotiating. Confirm deck configuration, loading method, securement, tarping, weather protection and unloading capability. A cheaper bid excluding required work is not an equivalent alternative.
  • Heavy haul—quote legal movement, not just transportation. Confirm dimensions, axle configuration, permits, escorts, route feasibility and jurisdiction-specific travel windows. Make conditional dates explicit until those dependencies are resolved.
  • All three—favor repeatable relationships over one-load extraction. Give qualified carriers complete information and prompt decisions. Dependable access to suitable equipment can be worth more than an isolated concession.

Negotiating discipline: Ask for a price against a defined service—not a discount against a national average.

💵 Fuel, Contribution and Customer Psychology

Diesel is $6.287/gallon. Retail relief is already underway, but the futures rebound makes continued relief uncertain.

  • Apply the contract rather than the forecast. Use the agreed fuel index, reference period and reset schedule. Customer and carrier adjustments may occur at different times.
