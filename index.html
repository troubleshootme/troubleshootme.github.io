  1. Audit unbooked van commitments first.

  2. Classify every uncovered shipment by its latest safe booking time.

  3. Set the purchasing ceiling before negotiation starts.

    • Use: Maximum carrier commitment = customer revenue − other direct costs − required contribution − risk allowance.

  4. Stop shopping when waiting threatens more value than it can save.

    • Compare the likely purchasing saving with the probability-weighted incremental cost of recovery.
    • Treat severe service consequences separately: a production interruption can outweigh an otherwise attractive expected saving.
    • If the executable truck exceeds the ceiling, escalate while appointment changes and customer choices remain available.

The behavioral trap today is anchoring: customers may anchor to the softer national headline while carriers price their particular truck’s operating constraints. Resolve the difference with shipment facts and confirmed alternatives—not a debate about whose market narrative is correct.

🧩 Partial Freight: Growth Must Earn Its Space and Time

Partial paid pricing exceeds posted pricing, while listings are expanding. That combination rewards disciplined shipment selection—not automatic consolidation.

  1. Establish the service model before quoting.

    • Terminal-network LTL and shared-truck partial service have different pricing and operating structures.
    • For network LTL, verify dimensions, weight, classification, packaging and applicable delivery services.
    • For shared-truck partials, confirm loading order, accessibility, compatibility and the complete stop sequence.

  2. Require positive incremental contribution after opportunity cost.

    • Include added carrier expense, distance, stop time, handling and administrative effort.

    • Confirm customer approval for shared-truck service and any transfers.

  3. Reject physically compatible freight that breaks the schedule.

    • A shipment can fit in the trailer yet force the original delivery beyond its receiving cutoff.
    • In that case, the added revenue must cover waiting, rescheduling and any displaced next assignment—not just the detour.
    • Measure added contribution per occupied trailer-day, not merely revenue per loaded mile.
