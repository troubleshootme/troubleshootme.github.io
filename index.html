⏱️ First 90 Minutes: Remove the Most Expensive Uncertainty

  1. Sort uncovered freight by its latest safe booking time.

    • Put rigid receiving appointments, temperature-sensitive freight and weather-dependent trucks first.
    • Identify assignments relying on a preceding delivery that has not finished.
    • Give each exposed shipment an owner, escalation deadline and qualified recovery option.

  2. Confirm the receiver before negotiating the truck.

    • Verify actual staffing, appointment validity and the latest acceptable arrival.
    • For weekend pickups, establish whether delivery is possible before Monday.
    • Obtain written responsibility for layover, temperature maintenance, redelivery and rescheduling where relevant.
    • A carrier concession is not a saving if an avoidable receiving failure consumes it.

  3. Run a controlled van procurement test.

    • Obtain comparable offers from established carriers before broadening the search.
    • Ask which approved operational change would improve the offer: a wider pickup window, earlier unloading or less empty positioning.
    • Change one variable at a time so the desk knows what created the saving.
    • Honor accepted commitments; apply revised pricing only within applicable quote and contract terms.

  4. Stop shopping when coverage risk exceeds likely savings.

    • Use the decision rule: keep shopping only while expected additional savings exceed the probability-weighted incremental cost of delay or failure.
    • Include recovery cost, service penalties, lost contribution and customer disruption.
    • Use your own comparable operating history for probabilities; this report does not supply defensible numerical odds.
    • Do not sacrifice a dependable truck to chase an unconfirmed cheaper offer.

🚆 Modal and Equipment Opportunities: Qualify Before Selling Savings

  1. Intermodal: sell confirmed availability, not nominal rail transit.

    • For flexible freight, compare truckload with complete door-to-door intermodal cost.
    • Confirm terminal cutoff, scheduled departure, destination availability and both drayage legs.
    • Include storage, additional handling and the cost of a missed cutoff.
    • Do not convert a month-end must-arrive shipment unless the actual service plan meets the deadline.

  2. LTL (Less Than Truckload) and shared-truck partials: keep the models separate.

    • Network LTL requires accurate dimensions, weight, classification, packaging and accessorial requirements.
    • Shared-truck partial service requires compatible cargo, workable loading order, available space and feasible appointments.
    • Accept consolidation only when incremental contribution remains positive after handling, detours and displaced capacity.

  3. Flatbed, specialized and heavy haul: eliminate scope gaps before comparing bids.

    • Confirm dimensions, weight, trailer configuration, loading method, securement and unloading capability.
    • For permitted freight, resolve route, escorts, site access and jurisdiction-specific movement windows.
    • A lower quote excluding necessary work is not a lower executable cost.

  4. Keep news-based capacity claims proportionate to the evidence.

    • No numerical OTRI (Outbound Tender Rejection Index) series is supplied.
    • The linked FMCSA regulations guide is compliance context, not evidence of refrigerated-carrier earnings or newly quantified capacity loss.
