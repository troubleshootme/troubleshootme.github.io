📦 Growth Desk: Partial Freight First, Specialized Selectively

Rising listings justify more discovery calls—not a presumption of accelerating rates.

  • Make partial freight pass a compatibility gate.

    • Confirm dimensions, weight, stackability, securement, commodity compatibility and usable trailer space.
    • Check loading order, stop sequence and whether any shipper requires exclusive use.
    • Distinguish shared-truck partial service from terminal-network LTL (Less Than Truckload); their pricing and service structures differ.
    • Include additional stops, route deviation, handling, detention and redelivery exposure.

  • Compare complete service plans, not mileage averages.

    • Obtain a partial option and a dedicated option where both are feasible.
    • Assess the customer’s delivery tolerance and cargo exposure alongside price.
    • Reject a nominal consolidation saving if one missed appointment can erase it.

  • Segment specialized demand before committing sourcing time.

    • Identify the exact trailer, operator qualification, handling method and positioning requirement.
    • Ask whether the customer has a released shipment, a project estimate or an unresolved equipment problem.
    • Treat the below-posted paid average as a reason to investigate comparable offers—not as an available purchasing discount.

  • Keep flatbed prospecting focused on repeatability.

    • Target shipments compatible with existing carrier relationships.
    • Confirm deck requirements, tarping, securement, loading orientation and unloading equipment.
    • A repeatable, well-documented movement may produce better contribution per desk hour than a higher-revenue project with unresolved scope.

💰 Quote the Whole Movement—and the Cost of Failure

Maximum carrier buy = customer revenue − other direct shipment costs − required contribution − approved risk allowance.

The risk allowance is an internal planning tool, not an incurred expense. Avoid counting fuel or accessorials twice.

  • Use complete trip offers.

    • Confirm whether fuel, stops, tarping, permits, escorts and other applicable charges are included.
    • Document detention, cancellation and changed-scope terms before dispatch.

  • Choose procurement posture by downside exposure.

    • For a rigid, high-consequence delivery, a slightly lower uncertain offer may be inferior to confirmed qualified coverage.
    • For flexible, low-consequence freight, continued sourcing may be worthwhile while preserving a workable fallback.
    • Use actual replacement options and customer consequences—not invented failure probabilities—to make that decision.

  • Turn operational certainty into negotiating leverage.

    • Offer accurate release times, responsive contacts and documented accessorial rules.
    • Ask whether an approved appointment change or reduced positioning improves the carrier’s offer.
    • Do not promise future volume unless the underlying customer commitment supports it.
