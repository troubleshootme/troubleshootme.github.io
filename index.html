  1. Build a consequence-ranked coverage list.

  2. Separate ready trucks from dependent trucks.

  4. Put a deadline on every conditional quote.

    • Link validity to the actual carrier offer and equipment position.
    • State what remains unresolved: release, receiving, routing, insurance or handling instructions.
    • Escalate before expiration rather than letting a conditional estimate become an assumed commitment.

Practical example: A lower-priced Atlanta-bound truck dependent on an uncertain preceding unload may be less attractive than a positioned truck with confirmed hours. Compare expected delivered cost—not the opening offer alone.

💰 Protect Contribution Without Damaging Carrier Relationships

Maximum carrier commitment = customer revenue − other direct shipment costs − required contribution − internal risk allowance.

Keep the risk allowance separate from actual incurred expense, and avoid counting fuel or accessorials twice.

  • Negotiate uncertainty before demanding a lower price.

    • Offer confirmed release, workable appointments, clear waiting terms and accurate shipment specifications.
    • Ask which operating change would improve the offer.
    • Carrier concessions are more sustainable when they come from reduced cost or risk rather than pressure alone.

  • Make fuel treatment explicit.

    • Diesel at $6.179/gallon remains a substantial cash requirement.
    • Flat supplied futures provide no fresh directional signal; they do not establish another imminent retail decline.
    • Apply the contract’s reference index and reset schedule. Do not mechanically reduce an all-in carrier agreement.

  • Define accessorial responsibility before dispatch.

    • Document detention, layover, cancellation, redelivery, washout and changed-scope terms as applicable.
    • Obtain customer authorization for additional service before exposure accumulates whenever practicable.
    • Ensure the carrier and customer agreements do not leave an unpriced gap.

  • Distinguish documentation problems from coverage problems.

    • A stale COI (Certificate of Insurance) warrants verification with the authorized insurance agent.
    • Check policy applicability, cargo value and relevant exclusions—not just the certificate’s headline limits.
    • Do not relax safety standards because a carrier has filed a challenge to a record. A pending challenge is not a cleared finding.

  • Verify unusually low offers without prejudging them.

    • Confirm carrier identity, authority, equipment, insurance and legal transit.
    • Independently verify unexpected contact, payment or destination changes through established channels.
    • A low price can reflect good positioning; it becomes useful only after executability is established.
