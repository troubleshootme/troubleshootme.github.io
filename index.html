💰 Quote Discipline: Trade Flexibility, Not Unfunded Margin

  1. Offer two service choices.

    • Appointment-protected: Price against accepted, qualified capacity and explicit accessorial terms.
    • Flexible: Tie savings to a wider pickup window, faster unloading or confirmed carrier positioning value.
    • This makes the service-cost trade-off visible instead of quietly transferring uncertainty to the brokerage.

  2. Shorten validity where density and momentum agree.

    • Consider 30–60-minute validity for uncovered Denver, Salt Lake City, Boise, Phoenix and Dallas shipments where practical.
    • Refresh after expiration or changes to shipment scope.
    • Quote validity is not a truck confirmation. Honor accepted commitments and clearly separate quotation from booked service.

  3. Negotiate the carrier’s operating plan, not just the headline rate.

    • Ask: “When are you empty, what must this delivery protect, and what would make this trip work better?”
    • Home-time alignment, a faster unload or a useful destination may matter more than a generic market argument.
    • Preserve dependable carrier relationships by trading real flexibility rather than demanding unsupported concessions.

  4. Keep fuel and local capacity economics separate.

    • Diesel is $5.864/gallon, with 14 consecutive daily declines in the supplied history.
    • Apply contractual surcharge indices and reset schedules.
    • Do not count fuel relief twice or assume it cancels empty miles, waiting or hot-origin scarcity.

  5. Set the customer price from complete shipment cost.

    • Include carrier purchase, known accessorials and shipment-specific contingency exposure.
    • For a gross-margin target: customer price = included cost ÷ (1 − target gross-margin rate).
    • Distinguish gross margin from markup and avoid duplicating contingencies already included in an all-in carrier purchase.

Customer language: “The pressure is concentrated in your pickup market, not nationwide. We can protect the appointment with confirmed capacity or test savings against a wider operating window.”
