⏱️ First 90 Minutes: Fix Exposure Before Chasing More Freight

  1. Build a shipment-level priority list.

    • Put temperature-sensitive loads, permit-dependent moves, strict receiving appointments and trucks with uncertain release times at the top.
    • Separate uncovered commitments, conditionally covered shipments and dispatch-ready shipments.
    • Assign an owner and latest safe decision time to every unresolved dependency.

  2. Refresh the purchases that matter most.

    • Start with reefer and other difficult-to-replace equipment.
    • Rebid routine, uncommitted van freight using identical shipment details.
    • Honor accepted customer commitments; escalate an unfavorable purchase internally rather than assuming the customer will absorb it.

  3. Verify the truck behind the offer.

    • Confirm actual equipment location, preceding delivery, legal hours and preparation requirements.
    • Verify carrier identity, authority, insurance and authorized contacts through established procedures.
    • Independently verify changes to pickup instructions, carrier identity or payment details. Urgency is not a reason to bypass controls.

  4. Set a stop-shopping rule.

    • Continue comparing offers while qualified alternatives remain and the dispatch window is protected.
    • Stop when further shopping risks losing the only feasible truck or the latest safe departure.
    • Escalate purchases below the shipment’s contribution floor before booking—not after delivery.

The right procurement metric is not the lowest quote received. It is the best executable purchase secured before service becomes endangered.

❄️ Reefer: Buy Commodity Compatibility Before Buying a Rate

  1. Make written handling instructions a booking requirement.

    • Obtain temperature, ventilation, sanitation, loading-condition and monitoring requirements from the shipper.
    • Confirm prior-cargo restrictions, washout requirements and equipment condition.
    • Include required preparation time in the pickup feasibility check.

  2. Treat sweet potatoes as a distinct handling problem.

    • Sweet potatoes are chilling-sensitive; a generic cold-produce setting can create a claim.
    • Do not assume pre-cooling requirements or combine them with colder-running grocery freight without explicit compatibility approval.
    • Resolve any conflict between loading instructions and carrier understanding before loading begins.

  3. Purchase the whole appointment chain.

    • Verify loading readiness, transit feasibility, receiving availability and equipment release.
    • Ask what happens if the receiver misses the appointment or cannot unload over the weekend.
    • Confirm who can approve detention, layover or rescheduling and how evidence must be submitted.

  4. Negotiate the constraint—not just the number.

    • Ask whether the carrier’s price reflects positioning, preparation, waiting, receiving risk or a weak onward market.
    • Offer a verified improvement where possible: a confirmed release, different appointment or compatible next shipment.
    • Do not promise a reload that has not been secured.

Procurement implication: The elevated reefer paid benchmark justifies earlier attention, not indiscriminate acceptance of high bids.

🚚 Van: Recover Negotiating Discipline Without Becoming Complacent

  • Remove yesterday’s price anchor. Today’s near-alignment between posted and paid averages supports testing current offers rather than carrying forward an assumed premium.
  • Compare like-for-like service. A lower offer with uncertain equipment release is not equivalent to a dispatch-ready truck.
  • Use flexibility selectively. Obtain customer approval for alternative appointments, then verify the carrier’s executable price before offering savings.
  • Keep difficult van freight in the early queue. Floor loading, extended loading times, multi-stop delivery and limited receiving windows can make an otherwise ordinary shipment expensive to replace.
  • Avoid over-shopping dependable carriers. Once a competitive, executable purchase protects service and contribution, marginal shopping can create more exposure than value.

Customer language: “We are testing current carrier offers against your actual loading and delivery requirements. Where flexibility produces a confirmed saving, we will quote that option separately.”

🏗️ Open Deck, Specialized and Partials: Protect Against Hidden Scope

  • Flatbed—make bids comparable. Confirm dimensions, weight, trailer configuration, securement, tarping, loading equipment and unloading responsibility. Aggregate pricing parity does not establish abundant suitable trucks.
  • Heavy haul—sell feasibility before a fixed execution promise. Resolve axle configuration, dimensions, permits, escorts and jurisdiction-specific movement windows. A Friday booking does not guarantee legal weekend movement.
  • Specialized—build competition within the actual capability. Compare qualified operators and exact trailer subtypes. If only one provider is executable, escalate that dependency before promising recovery options.
  • LTL (Less Than Truckload)/partial—distinguish the service models. Network LTL and truckload partial service can differ in handling, rating, transit and claims terms. Confirm dimensions, stackability, custody, accessorials and delivery commitments.

Consolidation test: Proceed only when savings survive additional stops, handling, waiting and service risk. More freight on the trailer does not automatically mean more contribution.

💵 Fuel, Contribution and Carrier Psychology

  • Keep fuel and linehaul negotiations separate. Diesel at $6.32/gallon remains a substantial carrier expense. Recent relief alone does not justify a broad rate reset.
  • Do not spend anticipated savings. Futures weakness may support further relief, but actual carrier purchases and contractual surcharge adjustments occur on different schedules.
  • Compare consistent all-in purchases. Identify whether fuel, tolls, stops, permits and expected waiting are included. Do not add fuel twice.
  • Trade useful certainty for better economics. Accurate loading information, realistic appointments and clear payment documentation can matter more to a carrier than repeated requests for a concession.
  • Measure contribution through delivery. Use customer revenue less carrier purchase, direct shipment costs and unrecovered accessorials. Track unresolved exposure separately until it is settled.

Behavioral trap to avoid: Customers may anchor to softer national pricing while carriers anchor to their most difficult recent movement. Bring both conversations back to this shipment’s actual constraints and executable alternatives.
