💰 Pricing and Negotiation: Give Customers Choices Without Giving Away Risk

  1. Shorten validity where density and rates align.

    • For uncovered Atlanta, Charlotte, Joliet and Phoenix shipments, consider 30–60-minute quote validity where commercially practical.
    • Refresh the purchase after expiration or any change in shipment requirements.

  2. Offer a firm-service option and a flexibility option.

    • Price the required appointment separately from an approved wider window.
    • Tie savings to an identifiable operating benefit: less waiting, better positioning or a compatible next pickup.

  3. Build the sale from complete cost and required contribution.

    • Include the carrier purchase and known shipment-specific costs.
    • When using a gross-margin target, distinguish it from markup: customer price = included cost ÷ (1 − target margin).
    • Follow the brokerage’s accounting policy and avoid counting an accessorial twice.

  4. Keep fuel economics separate from market leverage.

    • Diesel is $5.998/gallon, after 14 consecutive daily declines.
    • Apply contractual index and reset provisions. Do not automatically reduce both linehaul and fuel recovery for the same relief.
    • Falling fuel expense does not eliminate empty-mile, waiting or equipment-positioning costs.

  5. Challenge anchoring with executable alternatives.

    • A customer’s national-market headline does not price an urgent Atlanta pickup.
    • A carrier’s high historical rate does not establish today’s Cedar Rapids purchase.
    • Use qualified competing offers and service alternatives rather than arguing over broad market labels.

Customer language: “Your origin is tightening, but we can test savings with a wider loading window and a carrier that values the destination. The firm-service price protects the required appointment.”
