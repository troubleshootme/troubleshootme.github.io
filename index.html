  • Tightening trigger—qualified acceptance deteriorates. If workable trucks disappear or cancellations increase, shorten uncovered quote validity, secure critical capacity and request customer flexibility.
  • Easing trigger—better offers become executable. Capture savings when qualified carriers accept. Do not defend a premium solely because an older rate map rose sharply.
  • Disruption trigger—the itinerary breaks. Rebuild legal arrival, facility access and onward positioning before promising recovery.

Bottom line: protect service where density and rates agree, compete purchases where capacity is genuinely available, and monetize neighboring-market imbalances only when the truck’s next move works.
