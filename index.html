💰 Buy the Positioned Truck, Not the Cheapest Mileage Quote

High diesel makes proximity a commercial advantage—but avoid counting positioning twice.

At an assumed 6 miles per gallon, an additional 100 empty miles consumes approximately $106 in tractor fuel alone, calculated from $6.389/gallon. Driver time, maintenance and equipment expense are additional.

  • Use positioning to assess feasibility and negotiating leverage. If a carrier’s complete quote already includes its approach to pickup, do not add estimated deadhead fuel again.
  • Compare equivalent offers. The same pickup commitment, equipment, fuel treatment and accessorial scope must underpin each comparison.
  • Credit only confirmed reloads. Verify delivery completion, travel time to the next pickup, driver hours and cargo compatibility.
  • Keep speculative fuel relief out of today’s buy. Falling futures may ease later costs, but pump prices and contractual surcharge resets do not adjust instantly.

Illustrative decision: A nearby truck with a slightly higher complete quote may be the better choice than a distant truck with a lower quote if the latter has fragile pickup timing. The benefit is reduced failure exposure—not an automatic fuel saving to the broker.

Expected shipment contribution = customer revenue − complete carrier cost − other shipment-specific costs.

For uncertain shipments, also evaluate expected exception cost: the plausible cost of detention, layover, recovery equipment or missed appointments. Use your own operating history rather than inventing a probability.
