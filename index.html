  1. Rank uncovered freight by its latest safe booking time.

  3. Run a controlled van purchasing test.

    • Ask established carriers for comparable offers on freight with genuine appointment flexibility.

  4. Set the maximum commitment before negotiating.

    • Maximum carrier commitment = customer revenue − other direct costs − required contribution − risk allowance.
    • Include foreseeable waiting, permits, handling and unrecoverable accessorial exposure.
    • Do not count an unconfirmed reload or disputed detention reimbursement as earned value.

  5. Stop shopping when delay becomes the larger risk.

    • Compare likely additional savings with the probability-weighted incremental cost of failed coverage, recovery and service disruption.
    • Use your operating history to estimate probabilities; this report does not supply defensible numerical odds.
    • A service-critical shipment may justify early coverage even when flexible freight remains worth shopping.

🚦 Friday Decisions That Determine Monday’s Margin

  1. Price trailer occupancy—not just loaded miles.

    • A Friday food pickup with Monday-only receiving consumes equipment through the weekend.
    • Compare the complete carrier commitment, including waiting and temperature maintenance, against an approved alternative.
    • Moving the pickup only helps if the product, appointments and replacement truck remain available.

  2. Do not sell an unverified reload as part of the carrier’s compensation.

    • Confirm the reload’s release, location, equipment compatibility and legal timing.
    • Assign each confirmed truck and reload uniquely.
    • Price movements independently until the complete sequence is executable.

  3. Distinguish network LTL from shared-truck partial service.

    • LTL (Less Than Truckload) network quotes require accurate dimensions, weight, classification, packaging and delivery-service requirements.
    • Shared-truck partials require compatible cargo, available space, workable loading order and a feasible stop sequence.
    • Include additional handling, detour time and lost appointment flexibility.
    • Accept the partial only when incremental contribution remains positive after displaced capacity and schedule risk.

  4. Use two customer options instead of an unsupported discount.

    • Present the required service with confirmed capacity.
    • Where feasible, present a customer-approved flexible alternative with its actual cost and operational conditions.
    • This makes the trade-off explicit without promising that flexibility always produces savings.
